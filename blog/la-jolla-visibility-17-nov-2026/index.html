La Jolla conditions

La Jolla visibility today: a D, 5 to 9 feet, and the rain advisory got extended

The beta model calls Tuesday a D, medium confidence. The county did not lift the rain advisory at 8 a.m. like it planned. New target is Friday, Nov 20.

17 November 2026 · Dive Pro

The rain part

The county's general rain advisory did not lift this morning. It was supposed to come off around 8 a.m. Tuesday, Nov 17. More rain came through overnight, so San Diego County DEHQ pushed it out. The notice now lists Nov 17 as the most recent rain event. The new anticipated lift is Friday, Nov 20 around 8 a.m. That date is an estimate and it can move again. This is from the county's beach status feed as I could check it at about 9:15 a.m. Pacific Tuesday. It still listed the advisory on the La Jolla stations, including La Jolla Cove, Children's Pool, Avenida De La Playa, Vallecitos, Windansea and End of Marine St. Separate from the rain advisory, the Cove, Children's Pool and End of Marine St also have their own bacteria advisories that were up before this storm. The county's guidance is to stay out of the water during rain and for 72 hours after. Check sdbeachinfo.com (or 619-338-2073) before you go.

The short version

Tuesday, Nov 17. Dive Pro's beta model has La Jolla at a D, 5 to 9 feet of visibility, medium confidence, from the forecast issued at 8:49 a.m. Pacific. D is the single biggest bucket but it's not a runaway: D about 38 percent, F about 35 percent, C about 22 percent, B about 5 percent. So it's close to a coin flip between a D and an F, with an outside shot at a C. Odds, not a promise. A forecast, not a measured report.

Why it's mid

Mostly wind and chop, not a big groundswell. The main swell is about 5.5 feet at 6 seconds from the west, with a second one near 4 feet at 8 seconds from the west southwest. A swell at 6 seconds is short and steep, so it stirs the shallows. On top of that, the wind chop is another 5 feet or so at 5 seconds. Yesterday's swell was about 5.6 feet, so it's about the same as Monday, not an improvement. The model's best window is early to late morning before the wind builds, but the wind is already up this morning (see below), so don't count on a flat window. The nearshore water is about 74 degrees on the Scripps nearshore buoy, warm for mid-November.

Wind and tide

It's breezy all day. This morning runs about 14 to 17 mph, easing to the low teens by midday, with a bump back to about 15 mph mid-afternoon and gusts up to about 30 mph. It calms down tonight, under 10 mph after about 9 p.m. The tide is falling to a small low of about 3 feet at 9:32 a.m., climbs to a high near 3.8 feet around 2:30 p.m., then drops to a low under 1 foot near 9:45 p.m. Tide height alone doesn't tell you what the water looks like. Use it for timing.

Bottom line

Mid-to-low water, choppy, and the advisory is still on, now likely through Friday morning. If you go anyway, 5 to 9 feet is the model's range, and think twice about putting your face in runoff. More rain is possible later this week, so check the county board again before you plan around Friday. Full numbers are on the La Jolla forecast page. Dive Pro's model is beta and not validated.
