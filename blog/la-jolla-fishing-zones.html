La Jolla / Fishing rules

Same coast. Different fishing zones.

La Jolla's fishing rules change along the coast, and the fishing method matters as much as the location.

13 November 2026 · Dive Pro

You can dive in a protected area without being allowed to take fish there. You can also be in an area that allows a narrow kind of hook-and-line fishing but prohibits spearfishing. A general green/red fishing map can hide that difference.

This map covers the four marine protected areas around La Jolla, from north of Scripps to Pacific Beach. The colors answer one question: where do these MPAs prohibit recreational spearfishing?

Read the colors

Green is not a clearance

All four MPAs shown are red because none permits recreational spearfishing. That includes both conservation areas, even though each allows a specific hook-and-line exception.

Green means the water is outside these four MPAs. It does not mean every species, fishing method or beach entry is allowed. License requirements, species protections, size and bag limits, closures and local restrictions still apply. Swimmer locations can change during your dive.

The four areas

What changes from one zone to the next?

1. San Diego-Scripps Coastal SMCA. Recreational take is limited to northern anchovy, Pacific sardine, Pacific mackerel and jack mackerel by hook-and-line only. That exception does not permit recreational spearfishing or lobster take.

2. Matlahuayl SMR. No recreational take. The reserve and the Scripps conservation area share a boundary, so moving along the same stretch of coast does not preserve the same fishing rules.

3. South La Jolla SMR. No recreational take. This is the large nearshore protected area shown beside Bird Rock.

4. South La Jolla SMCA. Recreational hook-and-line take is allowed only for the pelagic finfish listed in CDFW's area rules: northern anchovy, barracudas, billfishes, dorado, Pacific herring, jack mackerel, Pacific mackerel, salmon, Pacific sardine, blue shark, salmon shark, shortfin mako shark, thresher shark, swordfish, tunas, Pacific bonito and yellowtail. The MPA exception does not override other species rules. It does not permit recreational spearfishing or lobster take.

SMR means State Marine Reserve. SMCA means State Marine Conservation Area.

Beyond the MPA boundary

Watch for swimmers

San Diego Municipal Code section 63.20.18 prohibits carrying a cocked or armed speargun in a public swimming area, within 50 feet of a swimmer in the water, or in any area where swimmers are present. Spears, barbs, prongs and similar implements must be sheathed, covered or removed there. The code defines an unarmed condition as harmless and incapable of projecting them.

A static map cannot tell you where swimmers will be when you arrive. Being in green water on this map is not permission to carry an armed speargun around them.

Before you take anything

Check the exact rules for your position

  1. Use the current CDFW map and boundary coordinates to check where you are. Do not navigate from this illustration.
  2. Read the rule for your actual fishing method and target species. A hook-and-line exception is not a spearfishing exception.
  3. Check current seasons, species protections, size and bag limits, license requirements and closures. Check local swimmer and beach restrictions too.

These colors describe take restrictions, not every rule about access or transit. CDFW generally allows transit through MPAs with catch under specific gear and surface conditions. Special closures can differ. Do not read a red area as a universal ban on being there, or a green area as a universal right to fish.
