La Jolla after the rain: county advisory at the Cove, vis a C

San Diego County put every La Jolla station under a rain advisory Friday morning. The beta model still calls Saturday a C, 10 to 14 feet, from Friday's 7:17 a.m. Pacific run, before the rain fully landed.

14 November 2026 · Dive Pro

Rain advisory first, then the vis

If you are kitting up in the Cove lot this morning, start with the advisory, not the vis grade. At 8 a.m. Pacific on Friday, Nov 13, San Diego County DEHQ put every La Jolla station under a rain advisory: La Jolla Cove, Children's Pool, Avenida De La Playa near the Shores, Vallecitos, Del Oro, Windansea, and End of Marine St. It is part of a coastwide general rain advisory from Oceanside to Imperial Beach after Friday's heavy rain and thunderstorms. The KSAN airport station logged heavy rain from roughly 3:50 to 6:35 a.m. Pacific. Under the county's standard guidance, the advisory lasts 72 hours after rain of 0.2 inch or more, so it runs roughly through Sunday morning, Nov 16, and it can extend if more rain falls. Runoff from storm drains and streets raises the bacteria risk. Avoid swallowing water, rinse off after you get out, and skip it if you have an open cut. That runoff also clouds the nearshore, so the C is likely the optimistic side, because the model run was built from Friday 7:17 a.m. Pacific data before the rain fully landed. Current status is at https://www.sdbeachinfo.com.

Saturday is still a C at 10 to 14 feet, with medium confidence. That row is in Friday's 10-day file, and the single-day forecast file is still Friday. About 69 percent of the odds sit on the C, about 23 percent on an F, and just under 8 percent on a B. You are looking at mid water, with a real chance of poor water and only a small chance of better.

Surf is about 7 feet, and the model says surf that size can stir up the shallows. The main swell is west-southwest, about 6 feet at 8 seconds, so the sets come in close together. A longer-period west-northwest swell of about 4 feet at 14 seconds is the one you can time.

Wind is already up, about 13 mph at 6 and 7 a.m., after about 22 mph around 3 a.m., with gusts near 30 mph. Early morning to late morning is still the better window, before the wind builds.

The model file shows 0.339 inches over the prior three days. That is the model's number, not a measured total for Friday morning's storm.

The newest Scripps Pier frame is Friday's 4 p.m. slot, captured at 4:20 p.m. Pacific. Grading was skipped, so there is no camera visibility grade.

The model has the water around 74 degrees. Full numbers are on the La Jolla forecast page.

The vis model is beta, prospective, not validated. Treat the grade as odds, not a promise.
