NOAA just ticked El Niño louder

Weekly numbers from 24 August. Odds, not a promise.

27 August 2026 · Dive Pro

As of 24 August 2026, NOAA’s Climate Prediction Center still has an El Niño Advisory in effect, and the latest weekly sea-surface numbers moved. NOAA’s weekly ENSO update lists Niño-3.4 at +1.8°C, Niño-3 at +2.5°C, and Niño-1+2 at +3.2°C. That is warmer than the July monthly values NOAA published on 13 August (+1.4°C in Niño-3.4). NOAA still gives a greater than 90% chance of a very strong event this fall and winter. Those are odds. They are not a guarantee that La Jolla gets a specific winter.

The Pacific does not wait for a monthly press conference. CPC updates the weekly Niño regions every Monday. This week’s table is the new fact. The 13 August diagnostic still stands as the official outlook. The weekly map is what actually moved.

On 13 August, NOAA said El Niño was strengthening. July’s monthly Niño-3.4 was +1.4°C, Niño-3 +1.7°C, Niño-1+2 +2.9°C. Eleven days later the weekly snapshot is +1.8, +2.5, and +3.2. Do not round that into a headline grade. It is one week of water, not the winter.

NOAA’s monthly outlook, still dated 13 August, puts a greater than 90% chance on a very strong event during Northern Hemisphere fall and winter 2026-27. For October–December, NOAA gives a 69% chance of a historic event, meaning a 3-month RONI of +2.5°C or more, which would exceed El Niño events back to 1950. NOAA is careful on the next sentence. Impacts consistent with El Niño get more likely at that size. They are not guaranteed.

La Jolla already had a warm lid before this week’s table printed. NOAA Fisheries’ California Current heatwave tracker, last updated 19 August, is still following NEP25A off central and southern California. That feature has been around since May 2025. NOAA says the extra heat of the last few months was mostly not El Niño. It also says the heating off southern California in the week of 15 August may finally be some more direct El Niño warming. Then it says the heatwave and El Niño may not add cleanly. The risk is duration. Warm, then still warm.

That is the local story, not a vis number. Daily feet stay on the forecast page. This post is the ocean’s larger gear.

For divers, the honest read is odds. A strong east-Pacific El Niño often means a warmer, more layered surface and weaker upwelling. Fish that like that water can hang around. Kelp that likes cold, nitrogen-rich water has a harder year. None of that is a promise for Saturday at the Cove. NOAA’s own next diagnostic is 10 September. Until then, the weekly table is the live wire.
