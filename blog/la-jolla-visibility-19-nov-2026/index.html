Sitting in the Cove lot. It's a D, and the rain advisory is still on

The county rain advisory is still on this morning. The posted grade is a D, 5 to 9 feet, low confidence, but the odds lean C. The lift estimate is Friday, Nov 20.

19 November 2026 · Dive Pro

The rain part

You're sitting in the Cove lot, and the rain advisory is still on, so that comes before the grade. San Diego County DEHQ still has a general rain advisory for the whole county coast, including La Jolla Cove, the Shores, Children's Pool, Windansea, and Marine St. The most recent rain was Nov 17, 2026. The guidance is to avoid swimming, surfing, and diving for 72 hours after rain. The anticipated lift is Friday, Nov 20, about 8:00 a.m. Pacific. That time is an estimate, and it can change. Cove FM-070 has had an advisory since Sept 29, and End of Marine St has had one since Nov 4. Check sdbeachinfo.com before you get in. A model grade does not cancel a rain advisory.

The short version

On Thursday, Nov 19, Dive Pro's beta model posts a D, 5 to 9 feet of visibility, middle about 7 feet, low confidence, from the forecast issued at 8:29 a.m. Pacific. The posted grade is a D, but the odds lean C at about 45 percent. F is about 22 percent, D is about 20 percent, and B is about 13 percent. The D that got posted is basically tied with an F, and a C is the grade the model thinks is most likely. That is a genuine toss-up. Odds, not a promise. It is a model estimate, not a measured report, and it does not cover entry safety or the surf.

Why it's mid

The swell is short, which is why the shallows stay cloudy. It is about 4 feet from the west, 263 degrees, at 5.8 seconds, so the waves are stacked close and they keep the sand moving instead of letting it settle. Surf is about 5.8 feet, so the beach is still getting stirred. The wind wave on top is about 2.6 feet at 3.5 seconds, local chop that wrinkles the surface while you try to look down at sand that has not settled.

Wind and tide

The wind builds through the day, so go early. It is about 6 to 8 mph through 9 a.m., about 10 mph at 10 a.m., and about 13 mph by noon, then about 15 to 17 mph in the afternoon with gusts up to about 25. Early morning to late morning is the window, before that wind stands the chop up and the surface gets harder to see through. The tide, from NOAA La Jolla station 9410230, was falling when the forecast was issued. Low is about 1.7 feet at 11:33 a.m. Pacific, and high is about 3.7 feet near 5:06 p.m. Pacific. Use it for timing the entry, not for guessing how far you can see.

Bottom line

If you go anyway, think twice about putting your face in runoff. Full numbers are on the La Jolla forecast page. The model is beta and not validated.
