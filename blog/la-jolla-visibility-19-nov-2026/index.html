La Jolla conditions

Sitting in the Cove lot. It's a D, and the rain advisory is still on

Beta model, low confidence, issued 8:29 a.m. Pacific. The posted grade is a D, 5 to 9 feet.

19 November 2026 · Dive Pro

The short version

You shut the engine off and look at the water. Short west swell. Waves stacked close. Visibility is 5 to 9 feet. The middle is about 7 feet.

Thursday, Nov 19. Dive Pro's beta model posts a D. A D is the poor grade. The water is mid or worse. Confidence is low. Low means the model is not settled on one letter. The forecast was issued at 8:29 a.m. Pacific. It is a model estimate, not a measured report. It does not cover entry safety. It does not cover the surf.

The most likely single grade is a C, about 45 percent. A C is moderate. D is about 20 percent. F is about 22 percent. F is the worst letter. B is about 13 percent. B is the good letter. Odds, not locks. The posted grade stays a D.

Why it's mid

The best window is early morning to late morning, before the wind builds.

Swell is about 4 feet. It is from the west, 263 degrees. Period is 5.8 seconds. Period is the seconds between waves. Short means the waves sit close. They stir the sand in the shallows. Surf is about 5.8 feet.

The short period wind wave is about 2.6 feet at 3.5 seconds. A wind wave is chop the local wind makes right here. It adds churn. Churn means the water near the beach gets mixed up.

Elevated surf stirs the shallows. Afternoon wind textures the surface. Textures means the top gets wrinkled. You look down through a bumpy window.

Wind and tide

Wind is light early. About 6 to 8 mph through 9 a.m. About 10 mph at 10 a.m. About 13 mph by noon. Afternoon is about 15 to 17 mph. Gusts up to about 25.

Tide is NOAA La Jolla station 9410230. It was falling at issuance. Low about 1.7 feet at 11:33 a.m. Pacific. High about 3.7 feet near 5:06 p.m. Pacific. Tide is the water level. Use it for timing. It does not tell you how far you can see.

The rain part

San Diego County DEHQ still has a general rain advisory for the whole county coast. That includes La Jolla Cove, the Shores, Children's Pool, Windansea, and Marine St. Most recent rain was Nov 17, 2026. The guidance is to avoid swimming, surfing, and diving for 72 hours after rain.

Anticipated lift is Nov 20, 2026, about 8:00 a.m. Pacific. That time is an estimate, and it can change. Cove FM-070 has had an older advisory since Sept 29. End of Marine St has had one since Nov 4. Check sdbeachinfo.com before you go.
